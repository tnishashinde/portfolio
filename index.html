Overview

ScholarPath is a university application management platform that simplifies a complex process. By consolidating deadlines, required documents, and progress tracking, it gives students a clear, organised path through their applications.

The Problem

Applying to several universities at once quickly becomes overwhelming. Deadlines, eligibility criteria, documents, and application updates are spread across different websites and platforms, making it hard to keep track of each university’s requirements or to know what deserves attention first.

The idea for ScholarPath came from my own experience. While juggling my university application deadlines with other responsibilities, I found it surprisingly difficult to keep everything organised in one place. Talking with friends showed me that many of them were struggling in the same way, which made it clear this was a common problem rather than a personal one.

ScholarPath grew out of that realisation. It aims to bring essential application information and tracking tools together on one organised platform, so students can move through the process with more clarity and confidence.

Design Goals

  • Centralise information: One dashboard for preferences, deadlines, requirements, and progress.
  • Improve deadline visibility: Highlight upcoming deadlines and show which applications come first.
  • Simplify documents: Make university-specific requirements easy to review and preparation easy to track.
  • Keep it intuitive: Reduce navigation and make current status and next steps obvious.

Outcome and Learnings

ScholarPath resulted in a platform that brings university preferences, deadlines, document requirements, and application progress together in one dashboard. Students can see which deadlines are coming up, what still needs to be prepared, and what to do next, without moving between multiple websites. Designing it taught me how much clarity depends on prioritisation: when users are managing many tasks at once, showing the right information at the right time matters more than showing everything. It also showed me the value of designing from a real, shared problem, since my own experience and conversations with friends shaped decisions about what to simplify and what to highlight. Along the way, I learned to reduce unnecessary navigation, structure information around the user’s current status, and balance functionality with simplicity.
